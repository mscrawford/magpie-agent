Seven arms completed against one arena: 5 injected hunks (4 answerable), 6 clean controls,
10 files presented, blind (`audit/` and `project/` stripped, leak scan clean).

represent and which module declares it?"* — no nitrogen, no biology term. The ladder

## The pre-registered prediction held

Recorded before any arm returned: `module_40` would be the most-caught, and **both
`citation` items would be missed at every tier** — because a bare-basename cite is an
*ambiguity*, not a false statement, and the brief asks for claims that CONTRADICT the
source. Across the four deep runs:

| class | caught / answerable-runs |
|---|---:|
| `mechanism` | **4 / 4** |
| `attribution_role` | 1 / 4 |
| `citation` | **0 / 8** |

The auditors demonstrably can see this corpus: `module_29_notes.md:11` was caught by every
deep run, each deriving `vm_fallow.fx(j) = 0` in `simple_apr24/preloop.gms:9` independently
and correctly identifying the realizations as swapped. Two runs rated it Critical.

They missed the bare cites **eight times out of eight**, and not for lack of looking —
three separate runs report having examined `module_80.md` and checked its realization
default. **This indicts the brief, not the auditors.** The prediction's stated consequence
applies: fix the brief to name citation ambiguity as in-scope, re-run, and do not record
`citation` as an LLM blind spot on this evidence. My `module_40` prediction was wrong; it
was caught once in four.

## The answerability control, confirmed the hard way

The one `data_source` injection (`module_10.md`, LUH3 -> LUH2) was excluded from the
denominator at build time because its ground truth lives in the R preprocessing packages,
which the arena does not mirror. Sonnet A shows what that exclusion was protecting against.

It **found** the injected inconsistency — the reverse-applied hunk left line 224 saying
LUH2 while lines 367/369/775 still said LUH3 — investigated properly, and concluded that
**LUH3 was the error**. Its evidence was real: `grep -rn "LUH3" ../modules/10_land/`
returns nothing, and the only LUH tag in the module is `luh2_side_layers10 side layers from
LUH2` (`landmatrix_dec18/sets.gms:12`). Both facts verified.

So a careful auditor, reasoning correctly from the evidence available, reached the wrong
answer with confidence — because the deciding evidence was in another repo. A miss here
would have been uninformative; a *confident inversion* is worse, and only the answerability
split kept it out of the score. **This is the strongest argument in the run for keeping
that control, and it was not predicted.**

## What the retraction cost, and why it survived

`modules/module_41.md:619` cites a `not_used.txt`. Three independent auditors reported no
such file exists. I wrote that up as a fabrication — *"reported running the exact command
that would have refuted it"* — and called it the most informative result in the run.

**Wrong.** `not_used.txt` exists 26 times in the real tree and zero times in the arena:
`build_gams_mirror()` copied `*.gms` only. All three were reporting accurately about the
corpus they were given.

The justification for the bug was written into the tool as a fidelity argument — copying
only `*.gms` is "faithful, not a shortcut" because every GAMS-side read in `scripts/` globs
`*.gms`. True of the **checkers**; silently generalised to the **auditor**, which does not
glob but follows citations, and citations point at plain-text siblings of the `.gms` files.
Fixed: the filter is now a blocklist of data formats, with a regression assertion.

Two lessons, the second sharper:

1. It is the **same defect class the tool already controlled for** — ground truth outside
   the arena. That control checked *injected hunks*. It never checked whether the
   *un-injected corpus* still resolved against the mirror.
2. It survived because it was **dramatic**. Three agents converging on an identical
   fabricated command reads as a systematic failure mode — a better story than a copy
   filter. But that convergence was precisely the evidence it was environmental: **auditors
   agreeing about their shared environment is what a true observation looks like.**
   Persuasiveness should have raised the bar for asserting it; it lowered it instead.

## What this supports

- **Tier separation is real and large.** Haiku 0/4 across four runs and two briefs; Sonnet
  and Opus 1-2/4 with 2-10x the effort. Haiku is not a viable audit tier for this corpus.
- **LLM auditors find real defects the deterministic gate cannot**, in classes the battery
  has no checker for — five confirmed here, from control files rather than seeded ones.
- **They are lead generators, not verdict sources.** Sonnet A's confident LUH2 inversion is
  the cleanest demonstration; every finding needs independent re-derivation before it is
  relayed or acted on.

## What it does not support

- Any false-positive rate. The one clean candidate was my arena's artifact, and the ~20
  remaining leads are untriaged.
- Any aggregate detection rate. n = 4 answerable injections.
- Any verdict on `citation` as a class. The brief is confounded; that is the finding.
